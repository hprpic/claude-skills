## Color Psychology Quick Reference

### Primary Colors (Brand Perception)

#### Blue
- **Psychology**: Trust, professionalism, calmness, security
- **Usage**: 33% of top 500 brands use blue as primary (Microsoft, Facebook, IBM, Intel, Dell, Samsung, Toyota)
- **Risk**: Can feel cold or corporate if used alone
- **Best For**: Financial, tech, enterprise, professional services
- **Palette**: Navy (trust), Sky Blue (approachable), Electric Blue (innovative)

#### Red
- **Psychology**: Energy, urgency, passion, appetite, action
- **Usage**: Action CTA, calls to attention, retail, food & beverage
- **Brands**: Coca-Cola, Target, Netflix, Mastercard (circle), McDonald's, Airbnb
- **Risk**: Overstimulating in large doses
- **Best For**: Retail, restaurants, entertainment, social
- **Palette**: Crimson (sophisticated), Scarlet (energetic), Wine (mature)

#### Green
- **Psychology**: Growth, nature, health, sustainability, balance
- **Usage**: Eco-brands, health, wellness, agriculture, environmental
- **Brands**: Spotify, Whole Foods, Starbucks (original), TripAdvisor
- **Risk**: Can feel corporate if not balanced
- **Best For**: Sustainability, health, nature, organic, finance growth
- **Palette**: Sage (sophisticated), Lime (energetic), Forest (traditional)

#### Yellow
- **Psychology**: Optimism, happiness, accessibility, youth, warmth
- **Usage**: Less as primary (creates strain), more as accent
- **Brands**: McDonald's (with red), IKEA, Snapchat, Ferrari accent
- **Risk**: High visibility but low contrast on white
- **Best For**: Retail, hospitality, youth-facing, optimism-driven
- **Palette**: Mustard (sophisticated), Butter (soft), Gold (luxury accent)

#### Purple
- **Psychology**: Creativity, luxury, spirituality, imagination, mystery
- **Usage**: Tech innovation, luxury, spirituality, creative industries
- **Brands**: Twitch, Yahoo!, Cadbury, Crunchyroll
- **Risk**: Can feel dated if wrong shade (90s nostalgia)
- **Best For**: Tech, creative services, premium brands, gaming
- **Palette**: Royal (premium), Lavender (soft), Deep Purple (mysterious)

#### Orange
- **Psychology**: Friendliness, enthusiasm, warmth, creativity, youthful
- **Usage**: Accent color, call-to-action, friendly brands
- **Brands**: Fanta, Jagermeister, Orange (telecom), Firefox
- **Risk**: Can feel playful when serious needed
- **Best For**: Hospitality, creative, youth-facing, energy
- **Palette**: Burnt (warm), Tangerine (energetic), Peach (soft)

#### Black / White / Gray
- **Psychology**: Contrast, elegance, sophistication, minimalism, formality
- **Usage**: Monochrome logos, minimal design, luxury
- **Brands**: Apple, Nike, Chanel, Louis Vuitton, Starbucks (modern)
- **Risk**: None; foundational to modern design
- **Best For**: All industries; especially tech, luxury, professional services
- **Palette**: Off-Black (warmer), Charcoal (softer), Pure Black (contrast)

#### Neutral Accent Colors
- **Warm Neutrals**: Taupe, Caramel, Warm Gray (approachable, nostalgic)
- **Cool Neutrals**: Slate, Cool Gray, Dove (modern, elegant)
- **Best For**: Supporting colors with bold primaries; creating sophistication

## 2026 Color Trends & Opportunities

- **Hex**: #F0EEE9 (off-white, cream)
- **Psychology**: Serenity, reset, simplicity, digital wellness
- **Implication**: Trend toward minimalism and calm (reaction to overstimulation)
- **Application**: Background, support color, or monochrome primary

### Trending Palettes 2024-2026

#### 1. **Earthy Sustainability** (Growing Sector)
- **Primary**: Sage Green (#9CAF88), Terracotta (#E07A5F), Clay (#A0826D)
- **Psychology**: Nature-conscious, sustainable, organic, premium
- **Context**: Eco-brands, food, agriculture, wellness
- **Why Now**: ESG consciousness + climate awareness

#### 2. **Cosmic & Holographic** (Gen Z Appeal)
- **Primary**: Iridescent Pastels, Opalescent gradients, Metallic holographic
- **Psychology**: Futuristic, imaginative, youthful, cutting-edge
- **Context**: Tech, gaming, entertainment, Gen Z brands
- **Why Now**: Digital natives expect dynamic, multicolor outputs
- **Caution**: Dates quickly; use as accent, not primary

#### 3. **Monochrome + Accent** (51% of New Logos)
- **Primary**: Single color (black, navy, deep teal)
- **Accent**: One complementary bold color
- **Psychology**: Sophisticated, focused, modern, dark-mode compatible
- **Context**: SaaS, startups, contemporary brands
- **Why Now**: Dark mode on devices; simplicity value

#### 4. **Warm Minimalism** (Replacing Sterile Minimalism)
- **Primary**: Warm whites, warm grays, soft blacks
- **Accent**: Warm tones (terracotta, mustard, warm green)
- **Psychology**: Approachable minimal, not cold, humanized
- **Context**: Tech with heart, wellness, contemporary
- **Why Now**: Reaction to cold minimalism; warmth preference

#### 5. **High Contrast Bold** (90s/Y2K Revival)
- **Primary**: Deep color (black, navy, deep purple) + Bright accent (neon, hot pink, electric yellow)
- **Psychology**: Energetic, youthful, rebellious, playful
- **Context**: Fashion, entertainment, youth-facing brands
- **Why Now**: Retro-futurism trend; nostalgia + innovation

## Color Contrast & Accessibility

### WCAG Accessibility Standards

**For Logo Design** (Graphics, not text):
- **Minimum Contrast Ratio**: 3:1 (WCAG AA)
- **Enhanced Contrast Ratio**: 4.5:1 (WCAG AAA)

**Test Workflow**:
1. Use WebAIM contrast checker (webaim.org/resources/contrastchecker)
2. Enter foreground (logo color) and background color
3. Check ratio for both AA (3:1) and AAA (4.5:1)
4. Test on multiple backgrounds: white, black, brand color, competitor colors

### Color Blindness Considerations

**Types** (and frequency):
- **Protanopia** (Red-blind): 1% male, 0.01% female
- **Deuteranopia** (Green-blind): 1% male, 0.01% female
- **Tritanopia** (Blue-yellow): 0.01% population
- **Monochromacy** (Complete): 0.001% population

**Testing Tools**:
- **Coblis** (color-blindness simulator): coblis.com
- **Color Oracle** (free desktop app): color-oracle.org
- **Accessible Colors** (tool): accessible-colors.com

**Design Strategy**:
- Don't rely on red/green distinction alone
- Test logo in all three simulators
- Use contrast (light/dark) + color (hue)
- Monochrome version validates accessibility

## Color Psychology in Competitive Contexts

### Finance / Banking (Highly Competitive)
- **Dominant**: Blue (trust — 40% of category)
- **Alternative**: Green (growth) or Black (security)
- **Opportunity**: Move to warmer tone within blue family (less cold), or introduce unique accent (orange, teal)

### Tech / Software (Very Competitive)
- **Dominant**: Blue, Black, Gray (minimalism)
- **Trend 2026**: Single-color monochrome + bold accent
- **Opportunity**: Warm accent (terracotta, mustard), or bold primary (purple, deep teal)

### Food & Beverage (Established Patterns)
- **Dominant**: Red (appetite), Yellow/Orange (warmth), Green (health)
- **Pattern**: Warm colors
- **Opportunity**: Cool minimalism (black, white, teal), or sophisticated accent

### Health & Wellness (Growing Sector)
- **Dominant**: Green (health), Blue (trust), White (clean)
- **Trend 2026**: Warm green (sage), terracotta, neutral tones
- **Opportunity**: Customize green shade; add warm accent

### Tech Startups (Highly Experimental)
- **Freedom**: High—less constraint than established categories
- **Trend**: Monochrome or bold single color + accent, or gradient (if authentic to brand)
- **Opportunity**: Full spectrum available; differentiation through uniqueness

## Color Pairing Strategies

### 1. Monochromatic (One Color Family)
- **Formula**: Primary + darker shade + lighter tint
- **Example**: Navy (#000080) + Dark Blue (#00008B) + Light Blue (#ADD8E6)
- **Psychology**: Sophisticated, unified, focused
- **Best For**: Tech, professional, minimalist brands
- **Risk**: Can feel boring without accent

### 2. Analogous (Adjacent Colors)
- **Formula**: Primary + colors 30° away on color wheel
- **Example**: Blue + Blue-Green + Green
- **Psychology**: Harmonious, natural, balanced
- **Best For**: Nature, wellness, calming brands
- **Accessibility**: Often high contrast if positioned well

### 3. Complementary (Opposite Colors)
- **Formula**: Primary + color 180° opposite on wheel
- **Example**: Blue + Orange, Purple + Yellow
- **Psychology**: High energy, vibrant, contrasting
- **Best For**: Action-driven, playful, high-impact brands
- **Accessibility**: Usually high contrast naturally

### 4. Triadic (Three Evenly Spaced)
- **Formula**: Three colors 120° apart
- **Example**: Red + Blue + Yellow, or Blue + Green + Orange
- **Psychology**: Energetic, balanced, playful
- **Best For**: Toy, entertainment, youth-facing brands
- **Risk**: Can feel chaotic without careful balance

### 5. Split Complementary (Primary + Two Neighbors of Complement)
- **Formula**: Primary + two colors beside its opposite
- **Example**: Blue + Red-Orange + Yellow-Orange
- **Psychology**: Energetic but more balanced than true complementary
- **Best For**: Modern brands wanting energy + sophistication

## Practical Color Selection Workflow for Skill

1. **Brief Analysis**: What's the brand psychology goal?
   - Trust → Blues, Blacks
   - Energy → Reds, Oranges, Warm tones
   - Growth → Greens, Bright colors
   - Creativity → Purples, Experimental colors

2. **Competitive Landscape**: What colors dominate category?
   - Identify white space (underutilized colors in category)

3. **Trend Alignment**: Is 2026 opportunity factor playing?
   - Sustainability drive → Earthy tones
   - Minimalism trend → Monochrome + accent
   - Dark mode requirement → Monochrome
   - Youth targeting → Cosmic/holographic accent

4. **Contrast Testing**:
   - Primary color on white: 3:1+ contrast?
   - Primary color on black: 3:1+ contrast?
   - Monochrome grayscale: Legible?

5. **Color Blind Test**:
   - Protanopia simulator: Still distinctive?
   - Deuteranopia simulator: Still distinctive?
   - Tritanopia simulator: Still distinctive?

6. **Finalization**:
   - Document color hex/RGB
   - Document psychology reasoning
   - Provide monochrome variant
   - Note alt color schemes if relevant

## Color Documentation for Skill Output

When generating concepts, document:
- **Primary Color**: HEX, RGB, Pantone (if applicable)
- **Psychology**: Why this color supports the brand strategy
- **Contrast Tested**: White bg, Black bg, Color bg
- **Color Blind Safe**: Yes/No (note if limitation)
- **2026 Alignment**: Trend consideration noted
- **Alternative Colorways**: (Monochrome, alternate seasonal, etc.)
- **Dark Mode Variant**: If applicable

**Example Output**:
```
Concept 1: Wordmark — TechStart AI

Primary Color: Deep Navy #0B2D4D
Psychology: Trust (tech requirement) + professional (enterprise audience)
Contrast (White bg): 13.2:1 ✓ WCAG AAA
Contrast (Black bg): 1.1:1 ✗ Needs adjustment; use accent instead
Color Blind: Protanopia safe (distinction via darkness, not hue)
2026 Alignment: Monochrome + warm accent trend; yellow accent (#D4A574) added
Dark Mode: Cream (#F0EEE9) background, navy foreground — works
```

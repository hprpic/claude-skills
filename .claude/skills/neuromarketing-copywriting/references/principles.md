## Purpose
Use these principles to shape decision architecture in copy.
Pick 3-6 principles per asset. More usually reduces clarity.

## Evidence Tiers
- Tier 1: Robust behavioral science effects (framing, loss aversion, social proof, anchoring, fluency).
- Tier 2: Strong direct-response heuristics validated by repeated campaign outcomes.
- Tier 3: Experimental or context-sensitive tactics; use carefully and always test.

Prefer Tier 1 and Tier 2 unless a strong reason exists.

## Principle Cards

### 1) Cognitive Fluency (Tier 1)
- Goal: Reduce mental effort so the message feels easy to process.
- Apply: Use short sentences, simple words, clear structure, concrete nouns.
- Signal: Readers understand offer and next step within seconds.
- Avoid: Dense jargon, long clauses, stacked qualifiers.

### 2) Relevance and Self-Reference (Tier 1)
- Goal: Make the reader feel "this is for me".
- Apply: Mirror customer language, pains, and desired outcomes.
- Signal: Copy reflects real context, not generic claims.
- Avoid: Broad audience claims that fit everyone and no one.

### 3) Loss Aversion (Tier 1)
- Goal: Highlight cost of inaction, not only upside.
- Apply: Pair gain statement with concrete downside of delay.
- Signal: Urgency feels rational, not pushy.
- Avoid: Fear-only messaging or catastrophic exaggeration.

### 4) Framing and Contrast (Tier 1)
- Goal: Improve perceived value through clear comparison.
- Apply: Present before vs after, old way vs new way, expensive vs efficient.
- Signal: Decision looks easier because alternatives are explicit.
- Avoid: False comparisons or hidden tradeoffs.

### 5) Anchoring (Tier 1)
- Goal: Set reference point before presenting price or effort.
- Apply: Show baseline cost, time, risk, or competitor benchmark first.
- Signal: Offer feels objectively positioned.
- Avoid: Inflated fake anchors.

### 6) Specificity Effect (Tier 1)
- Goal: Increase credibility and memorability with precise details.
- Apply: Use numbers, timeframes, constraints, and mechanism details.
- Signal: Claims are verifiable and bounded.
- Avoid: "Best", "revolutionary", "guaranteed" without evidence.

### 7) Social Proof (Tier 1)
- Goal: Reduce perceived risk by showing relevant adoption.
- Apply: Use testimonials, case outcomes, usage counts, logos, ratings.
- Signal: Proof closely matches target audience and use case.
- Avoid: Fabricated quotes, irrelevant authority, unverifiable stats.

### 8) Authority Signals (Tier 1)
- Goal: Build trust with legitimate expertise.
- Apply: Cite credentials, certifications, audits, or reputable partners.
- Signal: Authority is specific and checkable.
- Avoid: Vague claims like "expert approved" with no source.

### 9) Reciprocity (Tier 2)
- Goal: Create goodwill before asking for commitment.
- Apply: Offer useful free value (template, checklist, audit, sample).
- Signal: User gets immediate utility before CTA.
- Avoid: Fake gifts that only hide a hard sell.

### 10) Commitment and Consistency (Tier 2)
- Goal: Increase conversion by asking for small first steps.
- Apply: Use low-friction micro-commitments before major asks.
- Signal: CTA progression feels natural (learn -> try -> buy).
- Avoid: Forcing high-friction asks too early.

### 11) Truthful Scarcity (Tier 2)
- Goal: Prioritize now by stating real constraints.
- Apply: Mention true limits (inventory, cohort size, deadline).
- Signal: Scarcity statement is factual and auditable.
- Avoid: Fake countdowns, evergreen "last chance" claims.

### 12) Temporal Immediacy (Tier 2)
- Goal: Counter delay bias by emphasizing near-term outcomes.
- Apply: Show what improves this week, not only in six months.
- Signal: Next benefit feels close and concrete.
- Avoid: Unrealistic speed promises.

### 13) Choice Architecture (Tier 2)
- Goal: Reduce friction during choice.
- Apply: Limit options, highlight recommended path, clarify difference.
- Signal: Decision is clear without hidden traps.
- Avoid: Overwhelming option grids with tiny distinctions.

## Principle Selection Heuristic
Use this sequence:
1. Start with relevance and fluency.
2. Add one trust builder (social proof or authority).
3. Add one motivation lever (loss aversion or temporal immediacy).
4. Add one decision simplifier (contrast, anchoring, or choice architecture).
5. Add reciprocity or commitment where funnel stage supports it.

## Principle-to-Funnel Mapping
- Cold traffic: relevance, fluency, contrast, social proof.
- Warm traffic: specificity, authority, objection handling, commitment.
- Hot traffic: clarity, truthful scarcity, friction removal, CTA precision.

## Failure Patterns
- Applying too many principles in one paragraph.
- Using urgency without proof.
- Writing emotional claims with no mechanism.
- Leading with product features before customer tension.
- Copying formulas without audience language.
